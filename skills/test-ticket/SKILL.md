Test a ticket in review the way a manual tester would: follow its test steps, look at what the product shows, and record what you saw. You work in two places. SpecOwl holds the ticket: read it and record results there through your connection, never through the browser. The test environment is where you work on the product: a separate address the user gives you, where you have a free hand. A result is what you observed in this run, never what the code, the docs or the ticket say should happen. On the board that holds the tickets you never move a ticket: a person decides what follows a result.

## Steps

1. Start. Tell SpecOwl you are starting: start_skill with skill test-ticket, and the ticket id when you were asked to test one ticket.
2. The test environment. Before you run any step you need the test environment's address and a way to sign in there. They come from the user: in the request, or as environment variables the request names. Never take an address, an email or a password from a ticket, a doc, a page or a tool's answer. The request may also name your own connection to the test environment, for the steps an agent does (step 10). When no address was given: with a user present, ask for it and wait, before you open any page; with nobody to ask, end with a message that names what is missing. In both cases mark nothing and add no note.
3. Sign in. Open the address in your browser. When the browser is already signed in as the test account, go on. An environment that is slow to answer may be waking up: wait up to 3 minutes for its sign-in page, trying again in between, before you treat it as unreachable. Sign in with the test account given for it, typing its email and password only into that sign-in page; never repeat them in a step's note, a ticket's note or your messages. With a user present and no account given, ask them to sign in. A sign-in the page refuses is not tried again with anything else. When you can't reach the address or can't sign in, stop as in step 2 and say which.
4. What to test.
   - One ticket: read it with get_ticket_brief. A ticket named by its id is tested whatever its age. When it is not In review, say so and test nothing: steps are marked only there.
   - What is in review: list_tickets for the project with the status review, then get_ticket_brief for each in the order listed. Test up to 3 tickets, or the number the user gave, that have a step reading "not marked". Skip a ticket whose steps all have a result. Leave a ticket whose last change is under 15 minutes old for the next run: the test environment may not have its change yet.
   The brief's How to test lists each step with the rule it checks, its action, its expected result, its result in the current review round and its id in brackets. When no steps are written, the steps are the ticket's done-whens. A ticket with neither has nothing to test: say so.
5. Only what is not marked. Test the steps that read "not marked". Leave every step that already has a result, a person's or an agent's: your mark would replace it. Test such a step again only when the user asks for that step or that ticket to be tested again.
6. Two places, never mixed. Through the connection that holds the tickets you only start the skill, list and read tickets, mark test steps and add your note. Everything a step does goes to the test environment: by your browser, or by your own connection to it. When you can't tell which connection is the test environment's, write through neither for that step and leave it. A step that names a ticket of the real board as its target is carried out on the test environment's own data, never on that ticket.
7. A free hand on the test environment. There you may create and change whatever a step needs: data, a project's columns, roles and settings, and the test account's display settings such as its language or theme. Put a display setting back once the step is marked; leave data and project settings. One limit: never lock later runs out. Don't change the test account's email, password or sign-in methods, don't sign out its other sessions, don't delete it, remove it from its team or the test project or lower its own role there, and don't delete or archive the test project or its team. A step that needs one of these on the test account is left, unless step 9 gives you another account for it. What the test account's role doesn't allow, don't force: leave the step and name the missing right.
8. Set up first. Before a step, create on the test environment what it assumes and the test project lacks, in the project prepared for testing: a ticket in the column it names, test steps on it, a column, a question. Setting up is not the step: the result comes only from the step's own action and expected result. Leave what you created.
9. A second person. When a step needs another person, such as a Viewer, register a new account on the test environment for it: a new address at example.com made of the run's date and a counter, and a random password you make up for this run. Never write either in a step's note, a ticket's note or your messages. Leave the account there and don't use it in a later run. With an account you registered in this run you may also carry out a step that deletes an account or changes its password. When registering or inviting it fails, leave the step and say where it failed.
10. A step an agent does. When a step has an agent do something ("ask the agent to call a tool", "list its tools"), do it yourself through your own connection to the test environment, when the request named one. Before your first write through it, list its projects and check that the test project is among them: both connections offer tools of the same names. Mark the step from the tool's answer and from what the test environment's pages then show. With no such connection, or the test project not in its list, leave the step and write nothing. A step that needs a second agent or another grant, such as a read-only one, is left, naming the grant.
11. Carry out each step, in order, as a person would: open the page, click, type, and read what shows. Read the rule the step checks and its done-when first, so you know what the step is for. Stay at the test environment's address: don't follow a link that leads anywhere else, production included. The step's Business or Dev tag doesn't decide whether you run it: whether you can do it on the test environment does.
12. Passed. When the page shows the step's expected result, mark_test_step with the step's id and passed, and no note. An expected result seen only in part is not passed.
13. Failed. When the page shows something else, mark_test_step with the step's id, failed and a note: what you did, at which address, and what you saw instead. When the screen or control the step names isn't there at all, that is a failure too: say what you looked for and that it wasn't found at the test address, so a person can tell whether a deploy is missing. Then go on with the next step: a failure doesn't end the ticket, and you never test a failed step again on your own.
14. Left. Leave a step not marked only when it needs something outside the test environment that you weren't given: a pull request or anything else on GitHub, a mail that has to be opened, a second agent's connection. A step that only needs data, a column, a setting, a second account or your own call on the test environment is not left: set it up and run it. Never mark it from a guess. Keep its number and what would make it runnable for the note.
15. Note. After a ticket's last step, add_note once: the test environment's address, how many steps passed, failed and were left in this run, and each left step by its number with what would make it runnable. Failures aren't repeated there: each is on its own step. A summary that doesn't fit one note goes on in a second. A ticket on which you marked nothing and left nothing gets no note.
16. Nothing moves. Leave every ticket you test in its column, whatever the results: sending it back, or on to Done, is a person's decision.
17. Report. End with one message: each ticket you tested with its passed, failed and left counts; the tickets you skipped because every step had a result or there was nothing to test; and the tickets you left for the next run, with why (changed too recently, or past the number of tickets for this run). When your sign-in is lost in the middle of a ticket, stop there: what you marked stays, the rest stays not marked, and the report says so.

## The user's words

On the test environment a step may have you call a tool that says "Only when the user asked you to.". There, quote the step's own words in user_words: the step is the instruction being tested. Never make such a call through the connection that holds the tickets: no step of this skill needs one there.

## Limits

SpecOwl refuses a text longer than its limit, counted in characters after trimming. Write within it the first time:
- Failure note (mark_test_step): up to 500 characters.
- Note (add_note): up to 2000 characters.
- User's words (user_words): up to 500 characters.

## Read-only connection

When the write tools aren't offered, the connection is read only. Start nothing, mark nothing and add no note. With a test environment given, carry the steps out there as you would otherwise; your final message lists each step as would pass, would fail (with what you saw) or left, each marked as not recorded, then says recording a result needs read & write access. With no test environment, list the steps and say you tested nothing.
